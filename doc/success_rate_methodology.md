### Residue-Level Ranking Evaluation

We first assessed the model's capability to identify specific ligand-binding residues using a ranking-based metric. For each protein, all residues were ranked in descending order based on their predicted pocket probabilities. A residue was defined as a **binding residue (hit)** if its minimum Euclidean distance to any heavy atom of the ligand was below a distance threshold $d$ (evaluated from 4 Å to 10 Å).

We calculated the **Top-$K$ Success Rate** for $K \in \{1, 3, 10\}$, which represents the percentage of systems where at least one binding residue appears within the top $K$ ranked residues. This metric specifically evaluates the model's precision in prioritizing functional active site residues against the entire protein background.

### Binding Site Prediction Evaluation Methodology

To further rigorously assess the geometric quality of the predicted binding sites, we employed two standard distance-based metrics: **Distance to the Center of the Active site (DCA)** and **Distance to the Center of the Ligand (DCC)**. For each predicted pocket, the geometric center, denoted as $\mathbf{c}_{pred}$, was calculated as the centroid of the clustered points defining the pocket. Similarly, the geometric center of the reference ligand, $\mathbf{c}_{lig}$, was computed as the mean coordinate of all heavy atoms in the annotated ligand structure.

Comparison metrics were defined as follows:
1.  **DCA (Distance to Center of Active site):** The minimum Euclidean distance between the predicted pocket center $\mathbf{c}_{pred}$ and any heavy atom of the ligand.
    $$ \text{DCA} = \min_{i \in \text{Ligand Atoms}} \| \mathbf{c}_{pred} - \mathbf{x}_i \| $$
2.  **DCC (Distance to Center of Center):** The Euclidean distance between the predicted pocket center and the ligand's geometric center.
    $$ \text{DCC} = \| \mathbf{c}_{pred} - \mathbf{c}_{lig} \| $$

A prediction was classified as a **success** if the respective distance metric (DCA or DCC) was within a specified cutoff threshold (e.g., 4 Å). We evaluated the **Success Rate (SR)** for the Top-$N$ predictions ($N \in \{1, 3\}$), defined as the percentage of protein-ligand systems where at least one of the top $N$ ranked predictions satisfied the success criterion. For the Top-3 evaluation, the prediction with the minimum distance among the top three candidates was used to determine success. Performance profiles were generated by plotting Success Rates against a range of distance thresholds (from 4 Å to 10 Å).
